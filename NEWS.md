# pprof (development version)

- Work has started on pprof 2.0.0, a rewrite of the package's architecture that preserves its statistical behavior. All seven fitting functions of pprof 1.0.3 and the methods of their fits now run on the new code (below).
- The test suite now checks every exported function and every method except `print()` against frozen results of pprof 1.0.3 (`tests/testthat/fixtures/reference/`). These tests use the new suggested packages jsonlite and withr.
- New `fit_logistic_fe()` fits the logistic fixed-effect model from a formula, a data frame, and the name of the provider column, with the SerBIN or BAN algorithm of `logis_fe()`, whose estimates it reproduces exactly. Its object keeps the convergence diagnostics of every iteration and has `coef()`, `vcov()`, `nobs()`, `formula()`, `logLik()`, `fitted()`, `residuals()`, and `predict()` methods. It accepts transformed terms, interactions, and factor levels with spaces in the formula, and factor provider IDs when providers are excluded, which `logis_fe()` cannot fit. It warns with classed conditions when providers are excluded (with the correct count), when the iteration limit is reached, and when covariates are linearly dependent within providers, and rejects non-binary outcomes and non-positive `max_iter`, `tol`, and `effect_bound`. The rest of the new interface follows.
- New `test_coefficients()` tests the covariate coefficients of a `fit_logistic_fe()` model with the Wald, likelihood-ratio, or score test of `summary.logis_fe()`, whose results it reproduces exactly, and `confint()` gives their Wald intervals. Unlike `summary.logis_fe()`, the likelihood-ratio and score tests work for a model with two covariates; for a model with one covariate, and for a model whose refit with the default `min_provider_size = 10` would exclude providers, they fail with classed errors. They need the covariates: fit with `keep_data = TRUE`, or pass the data as `data`.
- New profiling functions for `fit_logistic_fe()` models reproduce the results of `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, and the funnel plot: `test_providers()` (exact, bootstrap, score, and Wald tests with flags), `provider_effects()` (provider effects with exact, score, or Wald intervals), `standardize_providers()` (indirect and direct ratios and rates with intervals), `funnel_limits()` (funnel-plot control limits and points), and `profile_providers()`, which collects them. Results are data frames keyed by provider ID in provider order, with integer flags. Unlike the old functions, they select providers stored as integers, work with factor IDs and any name of the provider column, keep each provider's interval limits with that provider when IDs are character strings, accept an integer `null`, reject `test = "robust_wald"` (which returned `NULL`), and report a standard score statistic that is not finite with a missing p-value and flag and a warning instead of failing. Wald tests and intervals warn only when they cover providers with no events or only events.
- New plots built from these results with ggplot2: `plot_funnel()` (funnel plot with one or more control limits), `plot_caterpillar()` (estimates with their intervals, flagged against a reference), and `plot_flags()` (shares of flags by provider-size group). `fit_logistic_fe()` models have `print()` and `summary()` methods, and `tidy()`, `glance()`, and `augment()` methods for models and results, re-exported from the generics package, which pprof now imports.
- `logis_fe()` and its `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, `summary()`, and `plot()` methods now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: formulas with transformed terms, interactions, and factor levels containing spaces work; with character provider IDs, `confint()` keeps each provider's limits with that provider and lists providers in order; `confint()` works with factor provider IDs and with provider columns not named `ProvID`; providers stored as integers can be selected with `parm`; an integer `null` works in every method; `summary(test = "lr")` and `"score"` work with two covariates; a standard score statistic that is not finite gives a missing p-value and flag with a warning instead of an error; `test = "robust_wald"` (which returned `NULL`), `backtrack` other than 0 or 1 with BAN, and non-positive `max.iter`, `tol`, `bound`, or `threads` raise errors instead of returning wrong results; the screening warning counts only the providers excluded; a fit that reaches the iteration limit says "not converged" and warns; and `plot(test = "exact")` raises a classed error. pROC's messages no longer appear during fitting.
- New `fit_logistic_firth()`, `fit_linear_fe()`, `fit_linear_re()`, `fit_logistic_re()`, `fit_linear_cre()`, and `fit_logistic_cre()` fit the models of `logis_firth()`, `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` from a formula, a data frame, and the name of the provider column, and reproduce their estimates. `fit_logistic_firth()` repeats the iterations of `logis_firth()` exactly, keeps the penalized log-likelihood and the convergence diagnostics, and works with every function for `fit_logistic_fe()` models. The random-effect and correlated random-effect fits use lme4's `lmer()` and `glmer()`, as the old functions do, and keep lme4's convergence messages.
- `test_providers()`, `provider_effects()`, `standardize_providers()`, `profile_providers()`, `test_coefficients()`, `confint()`, and `summary()` now work for `fit_linear_fe()`, `fit_linear_re()`, `fit_logistic_re()`, `fit_linear_cre()`, and `fit_logistic_cre()` models, and `funnel_limits()` for `fit_linear_fe()` models, with the results of the old methods: Wald tests and intervals with the distribution each old method used (normal or t), standardized differences for linear models and ratios and rates for logistic models (for random-effect models, the predicted outcomes over the expected ones), and the funnel limits of `plot.linear_fe()`. When `test` is not given, `test_providers()` and `profile_providers()` use the model's test (exact for logistic fixed effects, Wald otherwise). `standardize_providers()` records which sums its `observed` and `expected` columns hold (`indirect_numerator`, `direct_reference`), and random-effect models accept a numeric `null`.
- The `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, and `summary()` methods of `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` fits, and `plot()` of `linear_fe()` fits, now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: an integer `null` works in the `linear_fe()` methods; providers stored as integers can be selected with `parm` in the `linear_fe()` and `logis_cre()` methods; the `logis_re()` and `logis_cre()` intervals use one thread, where they used four whatever the user set; a `null` of several values, which the tests of random-effect and correlated random-effect fits and every summary applied by position, raises a classed error; and the summaries of random-effect and correlated random-effect fits work on objects without their lme4 fit. The help of these summaries says that `parm` selects the intercept as `"(intercept)"`.
- `logis_firth()`, `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: `logis_firth()` with `threads > 1` gives the result of one thread, where a data race stopped it after a few iterations; a singular information matrix, or a negative `bound`, makes `logis_firth()` raise an error instead of ending the R session; `logis_firth()` works with factor provider IDs when providers are excluded; in `logis_firth()`, non-positive `max.iter`, `tol`, `bound`, or `threads` and outcomes other than 0 and 1 raise errors instead of returning wrong results, the screening warning counts only the providers excluded, and a fit that reaches the iteration limit says "not converged" and warns; and in `linear_re()` and `logis_re()`, character provider IDs with a covariate matrix, and calls that match no input format, raise classed errors instead of failing in lme4, after a long computation on large data, or with "object 'fit_re' not found". The help of `logis_firth()` states the default `max.iter` of 1,000. pROC's messages no longer appear during fitting.
- `plot_funnel()`, `plot_caterpillar()`, and `plot_flags()` are rebuilt from shared parts and work on the results of every model: each flag has one colour and shape in every plot, whatever flags occur (lower orange, as expected blue, higher green); providers without a flag, such as every provider of a random-effect fit whose provider variance is estimated as 0, are shown as "no flag" (`plot_flags()` failed on such results); a subtitle states the test or interval, its level, and its alternative; one-sided intervals are drawn from their bound to the estimate, and infinite limits to the edge of the panel; and the provider-size groups of `plot_flags()` are labelled with their size ranges. `plot_funnel()`'s `line_size` is now `line_width`, `plot_caterpillar()` gains `line_width`, and the help of `plot_funnel()` describes the linear fixed-effect funnel too. New `plot_volume()` plots the standardized measures against provider volume, one panel per standardization and measure, coloured by the flags of a profile's tests.
- `caterpillar_plot()` and `bar_plot()` now run on the new code and draw the same plots as pprof 1.0.3, including colours that go to the flags present, with these changes: inputs they failed on without a message of their own (a matrix or tibble, a table without its attributes, sizes that do not split into the groups asked for) raise classed errors; they no longer trigger ggplot2 warnings about `element_line(size = )` and the unused `box.linetype`; and their help says that `bar_width` has no effect, how one-sided intervals are drawn, and which colour each flag gets. The horizontal `caterpillar_plot()` still uses `geom_errorbarh()`, which ggplot2 4.0 deprecates and whose `height` it translates to `width` with a message.
- pprof no longer depends on RcppParallel, Matrix, pROC, dplyr, magrittr, rlang, or tidyselect (Matrix is still installed with lme4, and the others with ggplot2, tibble, and caret), and building it no longer needs GNU make. pROC and logistf are suggested packages, used by the tests.
- `logis_fe()` fits are faster than in pprof 1.0.3, by roughly 30% to 65% with SerBIN and 20% to 30% with BAN in the package's benchmarks, with peak memory within 5% of pprof 1.0.3's. Its methods rebuild the new model from the old object on every call, which takes about 0.2 seconds per million observations, so methods that pprof 1.0.3 ran almost instantly, such as `test(fit, test = "wald")` and `summary(fit, test = "wald")`, are slower on large data. The new functions on a `fit_logistic_fe()` model do not have this cost.
- `linear_fe()` fits are about 50 times faster than in pprof 1.0.3 and use half the peak memory or less, because they demean within providers instead of building dense centering matrices: for example 0.08 s and 397 MB instead of 3.3 s and 801 MB on 100,000 observations of 1,000 providers. `logis_firth()` fits are 12% to 40% faster in the package's benchmarks. The random-effect and correlated random-effect fits take the time of their lme4 calls, as before. Attaching pprof briefly needs about 60 MB more memory than pprof 1.0.3 did, because its dependencies now load in a different order; the memory in use afterwards is the same.
- Most `test()`, `SM_output()`, and `confint()` calls on random-effect and correlated random-effect fits take less time than in pprof 1.0.3 in the package's benchmarks (about four times less for the tests and provider-effect intervals of random-effect fits on 100,000 observations), and the others at most 0.01 seconds more; `SM_output()` of `linear_fe()` fits is slightly faster. Like the methods of `logis_fe()` fits, all of these rebuild the new model from the old object on every call, so methods that pprof 1.0.3 ran in a millisecond or two, such as `summary()`, now take up to about 0.02 seconds on 100,000 observations.
- The compiled code no longer prints Armadillo's own warnings, such as "solve(): system is singular; attempting approx solution", to the console. No result changes; the new fitting functions will report such problems as R warnings.
- A developer interface for adding models is exported, ahead of the user-facing functions: `data_prepare()` prepares a model's data, `new_pprof_model()` builds the model object, and the model-contract generics (`provider_table()`, `expected_outcome()`, and others) connect a model to the profiling code. It is documented for developers in `?data_prepare`, `?new_pprof_model`, and `?model_contract`, and may still change before 2.0.0.
