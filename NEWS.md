# pprof 2.0.0

pprof 2.0.0 is a rewrite of the package's architecture that preserves its statistical behavior. All seven fitting functions of pprof 1.0.3 and the methods of their fits run on the new code and return the same objects and numbers, with the fixes listed below. The test suite checks every exported function and every method except `print()` against frozen results of pprof 1.0.3 (`tests/testthat/fixtures/reference/`). These tests use the new suggested packages jsonlite and withr.

## A new interface

- New `fit_logistic_fe()` fits the logistic fixed-effect model from a formula, a data frame, and the name of the provider column, with the SerBIN or BAN algorithm of `logis_fe()`, whose estimates it reproduces exactly. Its object keeps the convergence diagnostics of every iteration and has `coef()`, `vcov()`, `nobs()`, `formula()`, `logLik()`, `fitted()`, `residuals()`, and `predict()` methods. It accepts transformed terms, interactions, and factor levels with spaces in the formula, and factor provider IDs when providers are excluded, which `logis_fe()` cannot fit. It warns with classed conditions when providers are excluded (with the correct count), when the iteration limit is reached, and when covariates are linearly dependent within providers, and rejects non-binary outcomes and non-positive `max_iter`, `tol`, and `effect_bound`. The rest of the new interface follows.
- New `test_coefficients()` tests the covariate coefficients of a `fit_logistic_fe()` model with the Wald, likelihood-ratio, or score test of `summary.logis_fe()`, whose results it reproduces exactly, and `confint()` gives their Wald intervals. Unlike `summary.logis_fe()`, the likelihood-ratio and score tests work for a model with two covariates; for a model with one covariate, the likelihood-ratio test is computed against the model with provider effects only, as `summary.logis_fe()` computes it from R 4.5.0, and the score test fails with a classed error, where `summary.logis_fe()` fails; for a model whose refit with the default `min_provider_size = 10` would exclude providers, both fail with a classed error. They need the covariates: fit with `keep_data = TRUE`, or pass the data as `data`.
- New profiling functions for `fit_logistic_fe()` models reproduce the results of `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, and the funnel plot: `test_providers()` (exact, bootstrap, score, and Wald tests with flags), `provider_effects()` (provider effects with exact, score, or Wald intervals), `standardize_providers()` (indirect and direct ratios and rates with intervals), `funnel_limits()` (funnel-plot control limits and points), and `profile_providers()`, which collects them. Results are data frames keyed by provider ID in provider order, with integer flags. Unlike the old functions, they select providers stored as integers, work with factor IDs and any name of the provider column, keep each provider's interval limits with that provider when IDs are character strings, accept an integer `null`, reject `test = "robust_wald"` (which returned `NULL`), and report a standard score statistic that is not finite with a missing p-value and flag and a warning instead of failing. Wald tests and intervals warn only when they cover providers with no events or only events.
- New plots built from these results with ggplot2: `plot_funnel()` (funnel plot with one or more control limits), `plot_caterpillar()` (estimates with their intervals, flagged against a reference), and `plot_flags()` (shares of flags by provider-size group). `fit_logistic_fe()` models have `print()` and `summary()` methods, and `tidy()`, `glance()`, and `augment()` methods for models and results, re-exported from the generics package, which pprof now imports.
- New `fit_logistic_firth()`, `fit_linear_fe()`, `fit_linear_re()`, `fit_logistic_re()`, `fit_linear_cre()`, and `fit_logistic_cre()` fit the models of `logis_firth()`, `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` from a formula, a data frame, and the name of the provider column, and reproduce their estimates. `fit_logistic_firth()` repeats the iterations of `logis_firth()` exactly, keeps the penalized log-likelihood and the convergence diagnostics, and works with every function for `fit_logistic_fe()` models. The random-effect and correlated random-effect fits use lme4's `lmer()` and `glmer()`, as the old functions do, and keep lme4's convergence messages.
- `test_providers()`, `provider_effects()`, `standardize_providers()`, `profile_providers()`, `test_coefficients()`, `confint()`, and `summary()` now work for `fit_linear_fe()`, `fit_linear_re()`, `fit_logistic_re()`, `fit_linear_cre()`, and `fit_logistic_cre()` models, and `funnel_limits()` for `fit_linear_fe()` models, with the results of the old methods: Wald tests and intervals with the distribution each old method used (normal or t), standardized differences for linear models and ratios and rates for logistic models (for random-effect models, the predicted outcomes over the expected ones), and the funnel limits of `plot.linear_fe()`. When `test` is not given, `test_providers()` and `profile_providers()` use the model's test (exact for logistic fixed effects, Wald otherwise). `standardize_providers()` records which sums its `observed` and `expected` columns hold (`indirect_numerator`, `direct_reference`), and random-effect models accept a numeric `null`.
- `plot_funnel()`, `plot_caterpillar()`, and `plot_flags()` are rebuilt from shared parts and work on the results of every model: each flag has one colour and shape in every plot, whatever flags occur (lower orange, as expected blue, higher green); providers without a flag, such as every provider of a random-effect fit whose provider variance is estimated as 0, are shown as "no flag" (`plot_flags()` failed on such results); a subtitle states the test or interval, its level, and its alternative; one-sided intervals are drawn from their bound to the estimate, and infinite limits to the edge of the panel; and the provider-size groups of `plot_flags()` are labelled with their size ranges. `plot_funnel()`'s `line_size` is now `line_width`, `plot_caterpillar()` gains `line_width`, and the help of `plot_funnel()` describes the linear fixed-effect funnel too. New `plot_volume()` plots the standardized measures against provider volume, one panel per standardization and measure, coloured by the flags of a profile's tests.
- New `check_data()` checks the data of a model before fitting, as `data_check()` did, but reports instead of stopping at the first problem: the missing values of each variable, which the fitting functions drop; covariates with no variation or nearly none; pairs of covariates correlated above 0.9; and variance inflation factors of 10 or more.
- The fitting functions, the profiling functions, `test_coefficients()`, `summary()`, and `data_prepare()` of the new interface return their results visibly, so that they print when called without assigning the result.
- A developer interface for adding models is exported: `data_prepare()` prepares a model's data, `new_pprof_model()` builds the model object, and the model-contract generics (`provider_table()`, `expected_outcome()`, and others) connect a model to the profiling code. It is documented for developers in `?data_prepare`, `?new_pprof_model`, and `?model_contract`.

## Cox models

- New `fit_cox_stratified()` fits the Cox proportional hazards model stratified by provider, from a `Surv(time, status)` or `Surv(start, stop, status)` formula with `offset()` terms allowed, with Breslow (the default) or Efron ties, case weights, and the model-based variance or a robust variance with one cluster per observation or by a cluster column. The fits are survival's: they equal `survival::coxph()`'s with `timefix = FALSE`, and the robust variance equals `coxph()`'s for the same clusters while taking a fraction of its time. They follow pprof_py 0.7.0, the group's Python package, except where pprof_py's results differ from survival's, such as its robust variances with Breslow ties and delayed entry. Rows with weight 0 stay in the data and are left out of the fit, rows with missing values are dropped, and the status may be coded 0/1, logical, or 1/2, as `Surv()` accepts. Covariates the fit cannot estimate, such as covariates constant within every provider, raise a classed error naming them.
- Fits have `coef()`, `vcov()`, `nobs()` (the number of events), `logLik()`, `residuals()` (martingale, score, and dfbeta residuals), and `predict()` methods (linear predictors and risks, and cumulative hazards and survival curves for new data), and the new `baseline_hazard()` gives each provider's baseline cumulative hazard at covariates 0 and offset 0. `print()`, `summary()`, `tidy()`, `glance()` (with the events, AIC, and BIC), and `augment()` (with the expected events and the martingale residuals) describe them, and `test_coefficients()` and `confint()` give Wald tests and intervals, whose p-values are computed as upper tails so that very small ones are not reported as 0. The methods that need survival's own fit use the data kept with `keep_data = TRUE` or passed as `data`. The provider-level functions, such as `test_providers()` and `standardize_providers()`, are not yet available for Cox fits and raise a classed error.
- With a `Surv()` response, `check_data()` takes `weights` and `cluster` and reports what matters for a Cox fit: near-tied times, which survival's default `timefix` would merge and the fits keep apart; zero and negative weights; invalid statuses and times; providers without events or without person-time; covariates linearly dependent within providers; and covariates whose mean is large against their spread, such as a calendar year. `Surv()` is re-exported from survival, and `data_prepare()` prepares survival data with weights, clusters, and offsets for developers.

## Deprecated

- The functions of pprof 1.0.3 are deprecated: `logis_fe()`, `logis_firth()`, `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, `logis_cre()`, `test()`, `SM_output()`, `caterpillar_plot()`, `bar_plot()`, and `data_check()` each warn the first time they are called in a session, with the class `pprof_deprecated`, naming the function that replaces them. They keep working and give the same results; `suppressWarnings(expr, classes = "pprof_deprecated")` silences the warning. The `confint()`, `summary()`, `plot()`, and `print()` methods for their objects do not warn. "Migrating from pprof 1.0.3" (`vignette("migration", package = "pprof")`) shows how to move to the new interface.

## Changes in the functions of pprof 1.0.3

- `logis_fe()` and its `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, `summary()`, and `plot()` methods now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: formulas with transformed terms, interactions, and factor levels containing spaces work; with character provider IDs, `confint()` keeps each provider's limits with that provider and lists providers in order; `confint()` works with factor provider IDs and with provider columns not named `ProvID`; providers stored as integers can be selected with `parm`; an integer `null` works in every method; `summary(test = "lr")` and `"score"` work with two covariates, and with one covariate `summary(test = "lr")` tests against the model with provider effects only on every R version, as pprof 1.0.3 does from R 4.5.0 (before R 4.5.0 it failed), while `"score"` still fails, now with a classed error; a standard score statistic that is not finite gives a missing p-value and flag with a warning instead of an error; `test = "robust_wald"` (which returned `NULL`), `backtrack` other than 0 or 1 with BAN, and non-positive `max.iter`, `tol`, `bound`, or `threads` raise errors instead of returning wrong results; the screening warning counts only the providers excluded; a fit that reaches the iteration limit says "not converged" and warns; and `plot(test = "exact")` raises a classed error. pROC's messages no longer appear during fitting.
- `logis_firth()`, `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: `logis_firth()` with `threads > 1` gives the result of one thread, where a data race stopped it after a few iterations; a singular information matrix, or a negative `bound`, makes `logis_firth()` raise an error instead of ending the R session; `logis_firth()` works with factor provider IDs when providers are excluded; in `logis_firth()`, non-positive `max.iter`, `tol`, `bound`, or `threads` and outcomes other than 0 and 1 raise errors instead of returning wrong results, the screening warning counts only the providers excluded, and a fit that reaches the iteration limit says "not converged" and warns; and in `linear_re()` and `logis_re()`, character provider IDs with a covariate matrix, and calls that match no input format, raise classed errors instead of failing in lme4, after a long computation on large data, or with "object 'fit_re' not found". The help of `logis_firth()` states the default `max.iter` of 1,000. pROC's messages no longer appear during fitting.
- With no covariates, `logis_fe()` and `logis_firth()` raise an error on every R version. pprof 1.0.3 fails there before R 4.5.0, and from R 4.5.0 fits the provider effects alone, stopping after one iteration of its fitting algorithm.
- The `test()`, `SM_output()`, `confint()`, and `summary()` methods of `linear_fe()`, `linear_re()`, `logis_re()`, `linear_cre()`, and `logis_cre()` fits, and `plot()` of `linear_fe()` fits, now call the new functions and return the same objects and numbers as pprof 1.0.3, with these fixes: an integer `null` works in the `linear_fe()` methods; providers stored as integers can be selected with `parm` in the `linear_fe()` and `logis_cre()` methods; the `logis_re()` and `logis_cre()` intervals use one thread, where they used four whatever the user set; a `null` of several values, which the tests of random-effect and correlated random-effect fits and every summary applied by position, raises a classed error; and the summaries of random-effect and correlated random-effect fits work on objects without their lme4 fit. The help of these summaries says that `parm` selects the intercept as `"(intercept)"`.
- `caterpillar_plot()` and `bar_plot()` now run on the new code and draw the same plots as pprof 1.0.3, including colours that go to the flags present, with these changes: inputs they failed on without a message of their own (a matrix or tibble, a table without its attributes, sizes that do not split into the groups asked for) raise classed errors; they no longer trigger ggplot2 warnings about `element_line(size = )` and the unused `box.linetype`; and their help says that `bar_width` has no effect, how one-sided intervals are drawn, and which colour each flag gets. The horizontal `caterpillar_plot()` draws its bars with `geom_errorbar(orientation = "y")` instead of `geom_errorbarh()`, which ggplot2 4.0 deprecates: the bars are the same, and ggplot2's message that `height` was translated to `width` is gone. The plot data of `bar_plot()` are a data frame rather than a grouped tibble.
- `data_check()` now runs on the code of `check_data()`, with the same messages, warnings, and errors as in pprof 1.0.3 (its errors now have the class `pprof_error_data`), and is deprecated like the other functions of pprof 1.0.3. It completes its checks with a single covariate, where it stopped with an error at the correlation check.
- The compiled code no longer prints Armadillo's own warnings, such as "solve(): system is singular; attempting approx solution", to the console. No result changes; the new fitting functions will report such problems as R warnings.

## Performance

- `logis_fe()` fits are faster than in pprof 1.0.3, by about 45% to 75% with SerBIN and 25% to 50% with BAN in the package's benchmarks, and need less memory, except with many covariates: with 50 covariates and a million observations, the check that the covariates are not linearly dependent within providers, which pprof 1.0.3 does not make, raises the peak of R's memory by about 13%. Its methods rebuild the new model from the old object on every call, which takes about 0.25 seconds per million observations, so methods that pprof 1.0.3 ran almost instantly, such as `test(fit, test = "wald")` and `summary(fit, test = "wald")`, are slower on large data. The new functions on a `fit_logistic_fe()` model do not have this cost.
- `linear_fe()` fits are 35 to 55 times faster than in pprof 1.0.3 and need less memory, because they demean within providers instead of building dense centering matrices: for example 0.09 s and 276 MB instead of 3.2 s and 842 MB on 100,000 observations of 1,000 providers. `logis_firth()` fits are about 30% to 50% faster in the package's benchmarks. The random-effect and correlated random-effect fits take the time of their lme4 calls, as before. Attaching pprof needs less memory than pprof 1.0.3 did (about 60 MB less in the package's benchmarks), since it no longer loads caret and olsrr.
- Most `test()`, `SM_output()`, and `confint()` calls on random-effect and correlated random-effect fits take less time than in pprof 1.0.3 in the package's benchmarks (three to five times less for the tests and provider-effect intervals of random-effect fits on 100,000 observations), and the others about the same time, or up to 0.01 seconds more for calls that took a few milliseconds; `SM_output()` of `linear_fe()` fits takes about the same time. Like the methods of `logis_fe()` fits, all of these rebuild the new model from the old object on every call, so methods that pprof 1.0.3 ran in a millisecond or two, such as `summary()`, now take up to about 0.02 seconds on 100,000 observations.
- `fit_cox_stratified()` takes a fifth to a half of the time of survival's `coxph()` on the same data in the package's benchmarks, with robust variance a fifth to two-fifths. Its time is that of survival's fitter plus the preparation of the data from the formula and the data frame, which adds a third or more to the fitter's time at 100,000 to 1,000,000 observations with 20 covariates.
- In the package's benchmarks, `bar_plot()` and `caterpillar_plot()` take at most the time of pprof 1.0.3's, and `plot()` of a `logis_fe` fit, which rebuilds the model from the old object once, as the other methods do, about the same time; `plot()` of a `linear_fe` fit takes about 0.02 seconds more on 100,000 observations.

## Dependencies

- pprof no longer depends on RcppParallel, Matrix, pROC, dplyr, magrittr, rlang, or tidyselect (Matrix is still installed with lme4, and magrittr and rlang with ggplot2 and tibble), and building it no longer needs GNU make. pROC and logistf are suggested packages, used by the tests.
- pprof no longer depends on caret, olsrr, or globals: `check_data()` and `data_check()` compute the near-zero-variance rule of `caret::nearZeroVar()` and the variance inflation factors of `olsrr::ols_vif_tol()` themselves, with identical results. Attaching pprof no longer prints "Registered S3 method overwritten by 'car'". caret and olsrr are suggested packages, used by the tests that compare these results. pprof now depends, directly or through other packages, on 39 packages other than R's base packages, survival among them, instead of 130.
- pprof imports survival (3.5-8 or later), whose fitters fit the Cox models.
- pprof requires ggplot2 4.0.0 or later, under which its plots were compared with those of pprof 1.0.3.
- pprof requires R 4.4.0 or later, where pprof 1.0.3 declared R 4.1.0. With the current versions of its dependencies, R 4.1 cannot install them, and on R 4.2 and 4.3 printing a model fitted with lme4 fails in the reformulas package, which calls a function that R added in version 4.4.0.

## Documentation

- New vignettes replace those of pprof 1.0.3, which described functions that do not exist and in places contradicted the code: "Getting started with pprof" (`vignette("pprof", package = "pprof")`), "Models and what they support", "Statistical methods" (the formulas behind every result, including how often the tests of random-effect models flag providers and how often their intervals cover the effects), "Migrating from pprof 1.0.3", and "Adding a model to pprof". They are built and checked with the package.
- The help pages of the new interface have examples, link to the related functions, and say how providers are ordered, and the package has a help page, `?pprof`. The help of `ExampleDataBinary` gives its size correctly (7,944 observations, not 7,994), the help of `data_check()` says that it stops when a value is missing while the fitting functions drop incomplete rows, and the help of `test_providers()` no longer says that the estimates of providers without events or with only events sit at the effect bound (they are where the iterations stopped). The README describes the new interface.
- The configuration of the package website is rewritten for the new interface: the reference index groups the help pages by task, with the interface of pprof 1.0.3 last, the vignettes are its articles, and the addresses of the old articles redirect to the new vignettes. The site is built by the package's continuous integration and is no longer kept in the repository.
