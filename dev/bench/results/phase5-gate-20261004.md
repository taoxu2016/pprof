## Summary

- No task regresses in time. The one task the comparison flags, the `logis_re` fit on `bin-1e5-m1000-p5` (61.9 s against the baseline's 52.8 s), takes the reference's time when the two are measured alternately (median ratios 0.995 and 1.01, fastest 0.998 and 1.01): the reference itself now takes 61.3 to 61.7 s, 16% to 17% more than two days earlier, the drift DEC-038 guards against.
- Of the 32 methods Phase 5 replaced that both runs measured, 20 are faster than the reference's (up to 4.6 times: the RE tests and provider-effect intervals on 1e5 observations) and 12 slower by at most 0.016 s; `plot()` of a `linear_fe` fit is 0.028 s slower (0.080 s against 0.108 s, paired). All are below DEC-023's 0.05 s threshold. The slower ones are calls that pprof 1.0.3 answered from stored numbers in a few milliseconds, such as `summary()`: the compatibility methods rebuild the model on every call (DEC-039, DEC-049).
- Every memory flag is the transient of the dependencies' load order accepted at the Phase 4 gate (DEC-044): the extra peak is in place before each task runs, the same as in the Phase 4 tree, and the tasks themselves add at most 11.6 MB more than the reference's (16 MB for the linear FE plot, whose total peak is half the reference's); R's allocations in the paired tasks are within 1% of the reference's.
- DEC-044's other exception, `logis_fe` with 50 covariates on 1e6 observations, is not flagged this time (8.5% above the baseline).
- Both runs failed to measure `plot()` of a `linear_fe` fit, a harness problem in the reference and the working tree alike, now fixed (`29ef593`); the paired run measured it.

## The comparison

`comparison-20261004-windows-phase5.md` compares 79 tasks (two of the 81 were not measured in both runs: `linear_fe` on `lin-1e5-m100-p5`, skipped in every run because the reference's dense centering matrices would not fit in memory, as in the baseline; and `plot()` of a `linear_fe` fit, below). It flags 56 tasks: one in time, `logis_re` on `bin-1e5-m1000-p5`, and 56 in peak memory (the time-flagged task among them); one more, `logis_cre` on `bin-1e4-m100-p5`, has a median 18% above the baseline's and a fastest run 3% above, which DEC-023 marks for a rerun.

### Time

`logis_re` on `bin-1e5-m1000-p5` took 61.9 s against the baseline's 52.8 s (median and fastest ratios 1.17). Phase 5 did not change the RE fits, which spend their time in lme4's `glmer()` (DEC-042). Measured alternately with the reference, the two take the same time (below): the reference itself takes 61.3 to 61.7 s today, 16% to 17% more than in the baseline two days earlier. Phase 5 changed no fitting code (`R/compat-fits.R` and the engines are untouched, and the model files changed only in their family specifications), and the other fits show the same session drift against the Phase 4 gate's run of the working tree: over the 44 earlier tasks other than the three linear FE methods of the next section, the Phase 5 tree's medians are 0.76 to 1.37 times the Phase 4 tree's (half of them within 0.94 to 1.02). The largest ratios are fits: `logis_fe` on `bin-1e4-m100-p5` (0.033 s then, 0.045 s now, the reference's 0.046 s), on `bin-1e5-m1000-p5-skewed` (0.33 s, 0.40 s, the reference's 0.86 s), and with 50 covariates (30.4 s, 35.3 s, the reference's 44.0 s), and `logis_cre` on `bin-1e4-m100-p5` (8.6 s, 10.1 s), the rerun above. None of them is flagged against the reference.

### The methods Phase 5 replaced

Of the 32 Phase 5 tasks measured in both runs, 20 are faster than the reference's and 12 slower, by at most 0.016 s, below DEC-023's 0.05 s threshold:

| Tasks | Reference median s | Working tree median s | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| `test()` and `confint(option = "alpha")` of logistic RE, linear RE, and linear CRE fits; `SM_output()` and `confint(option = "SM")` of linear RE and CRE fits on 1e4 observations (14 tasks) | 0.006 to 0.100 | 0.003 to 0.024 | 0.22 to 0.58 |
| `SM_output()` and `confint(option = "SM")` of logistic RE fits, and of linear RE fits on 1e5 observations (6 tasks) | 0.055 to 10.3 | 0.041 to 9.89 | 0.66 to 0.96 |
| `SM_output()` and `confint(option = "SM")` of a logistic CRE fit on 1e4 observations | 0.035, 0.107 | 0.040, 0.110 | 1.14, 1.03 |
| `summary()` of RE and CRE fits, and `test()` and `confint(option = "alpha")` of a logistic CRE fit (8 tasks) | 0.0016 to 0.0019 | 0.0036 to 0.0166 | 1.9 to 9.1 |
| `summary()` and `confint(option = "gamma")` of a `linear_fe` fit on 1e5 observations | 0.0005, 0.0035 | 0.016, 0.017 | 33, 4.7 |

The slower calls are those that pprof 1.0.3 ran in a few milliseconds from stored numbers: the compatibility methods rebuild the new model from the old object on every call (DEC-039, extended to these methods by DEC-049), which takes about 0.015 s on 100,000 observations. The same holds for the earlier linear FE tasks, which the Phase 4 tree still ran with the reference's code: on `lin-1e5-m1000-p5`, `test()` takes 0.017 s against 0.004 s and `confint()` 0.031 s against 0.021 s, while `SM_output()` (both standardizations) is faster, 0.38 s against 0.42 s.

### Peak memory

Every memory flag has the cause accepted at the Phase 4 gate (DEC-044): the transient of the dependencies' load order. The extra peak is in place before each task runs, 51 to 79 MB in the flagged tasks, and the Phase 5 tree's peak before each task equals the Phase 4 tree's (over the 47 earlier tasks, a median difference of −0.5 MB). What the tasks themselves add differs from the reference by at most 11.6 MB (`SM_output()` and `confint(option = "SM")` of a linear RE fit on 1e5 observations: 34 and 38 MB against 23 and 26 MB). The `linear_fe` methods use half the reference's peak, because the fit made before them is the new, smaller one (402 to 420 MB against 841 to 844 MB).

DEC-044's other exception, `logis_fe` with 50 covariates on 1e6 observations, is not flagged this time: 2280 MB against the baseline's 2102 MB (8.5%), within the 360 MB by which the process peak of that task varied between runs at the Phase 4 gate; it remains to be investigated in Phase 8 (DEC-045).

## `plot()` of a `linear_fe` fit

Both runs failed on this task with bench's "Memory profiling failed": `bench::mark()` profiles the allocations of the plot's ggplot2 code in an extra run and cannot parse the result, in the reference and the working tree alike. The harness now times such calls without profiling and records their allocations as missing (`29ef593`; no other task changes), and the task was measured in the paired run below.
