## The MUST: at most 10% slower than the engine calls (DEC-105)

A fit is too slow when, in every round, its median and fastest runs are more than 10% above its engine calls plus the preparation of their inputs, with its median at least 0.05 s above (DEC-038, DEC-105). In every run, every fit's estimates equal its engine calls' bitwise.

| Run | Fits | Too slow (DEC-105) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| C2 | 21 | 0 | 17 too slow against the engine call alone, which DEC-105 resolved: the fit's time beyond the fitter is the preparation of its inputs from the formula and data frame (27% to 33% of the fitter's time at 20 covariates, `dev/design/coxph-facts/21`) |
| C3 | 21 | 2 | providers-7500 Breslow and covariates-5 robust, at the rule's line: in one process 1.107 and 1.087 times the comparator, the excess being `standardize_providers()`'s table of 7,500 providers (0.048 s; fact 25); accepted for C3 at its gate |

## The SHOULD: no slower than pprof_py (DEC-098)

DEC-098 reads the SHOULD as applying to the robust variance, the measures, and the tests, not to large fits. pprof_py's times are from the project lead's Windows machine (C1, and C5 for the Fine–Gray model), on numba's 8 threads where it uses them, so ratios beside a runner's times are indicative.

- **Fits.** pprof_py fits 1,000,000 rows in 4.3 s against survival's `agreg.fit()`'s 17.2 s (C1); the package's fits take 0.2 to 0.5 of `coxph()`'s time on the same data (C2). Not met at large sizes, as DEC-098 allows.
- **The robust variance.** survival's robust step takes 0.07 to 0.23 s at 100,000 rows with 5 or 20 covariates, 0.6 to 0.9 s with 50, and 2.0 to 2.4 s at 1,000,000 rows, where pprof_py's adds 0 to 0.2 s and 0.9 s (C2): met with 5 covariates only.
- **The measures.** 0.80 s against pprof_py's 1.32 s at 1,000,000 rows, about as long at 100,000 rows with 100 or 1,000 providers (0.07 to 0.10 s against 0.09 to 0.12 s), and longer at 10,000 rows (0.028 s against 0.009 s) and with 7,500 providers (0.20 s against 0.13 s) (C3's `profile` task). Not met in those two scenarios.
- **The tests.** The mid-p test with its limits is 20 to 600 times faster than pprof_py's (C3; 256 times on the corner, C3 runner), after DEC-110's root search; the exact test with its limits is faster everywhere but at 10,000 rows, where both take under 0.02 s.
- **The elastic-net model.** The path 4.5 times faster than pprof_py's (19.9 s against 89.9 s), and the path with its cross-validation 9.3 times faster (about 185 s against 1,717 s) (C4).
- **The Fine–Gray model.** On the C6 runner the fit takes 5.35 s on center against pprof_py's 3.82 s, 3.86 s against 3.27 s with every entry at 0, and 5.61 s against 5.04 s on providers-7500 with every entry at 0; on the project lead's machine (C5, paging) 10.5 s and 8.0 s on center. pprof_py raises on covariates-5 (D-80), which the fit takes in 4.2 s. Not met, as DEC-098 allows (DEC-122).

## The brief's other items

- **Expected counts in closed form** (K-137): 0.003 s at 10,000 rows to 0.29 s at 1,000,000 rows (C3's `profile` task), computed once with the fit.
- **The formula interface's cost** (DEC-100, DEC-105): `data_prepare()` and the chunks' data frames are timed beside the engine calls in every paired run; on the C6 runner they take 0.11 to 0.43 s of the Fine–Gray fits' 3.8 to 24.8 s.
- **Against the user's calls.** The stratified fits take 0.2 to 0.5 of `coxph()`'s time (C2). The Fine–Gray fits take 0.21 to 0.23 of the time of one stratified `finegray()` call followed by `coxph(cluster = subject)` on center and covariates-5, 0.50 on providers-100, and 0.058 on providers-7500 (C6 runner), with the same estimates.
- **Memory.** Peak memory is recorded, not gated (DEC-004). The Fine–Gray fit's object holds about 5.4 to 6.1 MB for 100,000 observations; its process peaks at 0.9 to 1.4 GB on four scenarios and 5.1 GB on providers-100, whose expansion has 4.3 million rows, against 1.0 to 2.2 GB and 9.4 GB for the user's calls (C6 runner).
